⚠️ Photobiomodulation Safety & Biphasic Dose Alert

The Arndt-Schulz Law (Biphasic Dose Curve): In phototherapy, more is not better. Lower doses (3–15 J/cm²) stimulate mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase and ATP production, whereas excessive doses (> 35 J/cm²) cause phototoxicity and cellular inhibition.

Retinal Protection: High-powered near-infrared (850nm) and deep red (660nm) LEDs can cause permanent thermal retinal damage. Always wear certified phototherapy eye protection goggles during face exposure.

Contraindications: Do not shine photobiomodulation devices directly over confirmed malignant melanoma, suspicious skin tumors, active thyroid gland nodules, or if taking photosensitizing medications (e.g., Accutane, doxycycline).

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate red light therapy treatment time?
Use the standard photobiology formula: Session Time (seconds) = [Target Fluence (Joules/cm²) × 1000] ÷ Irradiance (mW/cm²). For instance, delivering 6 J/cm² with a panel measuring 50 mW/cm² requires (6 × 1000) / 50 = 120 seconds (2 minutes).
Can I overdo red light therapy?
Yes. Due to the biphasic dose response (Arndt-Schulz curve), excessive light exposure (>30 J/cm² on a single area) triggers phototoxicity and reduces cellular ATP synthesis. Sticking to 4–15 J/cm² yields optimal bio-stimulatory results.
Do clothes block red light wavelengths?
Red light (660nm) is significantly reflected and absorbed by standard fabrics. Near-infrared light (850nm) can partially penetrate thin light-colored synthetic fabrics, but direct bare skin exposure is universally recommended for optimal photon absorption.
